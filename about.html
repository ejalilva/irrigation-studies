I’m a research scientist in the Hydrological Sciences Laboratory at NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center. I bring human water use, above all irrigation, into water-cycle models: I use satellite observations and machine learning to learn the decisions farmers make, and build them into NASA’s Land Information System.

01What I work on

Large-scale water-cycle models usually treat human demand for water as a fixed input. People don’t behave that way: farmers decide when and how much to irrigate. I want models to treat those decisions as a dynamic part of the system, learned from satellite observations, so the field can move toward modeling people and water together.

That has meant three questions about irrigation: where it happens, when, and how much water it uses. Each answer has raised the next one; the irrigation studies tell that story.

  • Hydrological and land surface modeling
  • Satellite data assimilation
  • Soil moisture remote sensing
  • Irrigation mapping and quantification
  • Machine learning and geospatial foundation models

02How I got here

Curiosity, some luck and good collaborators.

I didn’t always know I would work on hydrology. In civil engineering, a course in GIS and remote sensing, built on teaching materials Dr. David Maidment had shared openly, got me hooked on watching water from space. Years later I could thank him in person, when he visited Goddard in November 2024.

During my PhD at Sharif University of Technology, an email to Luca Brocca led to research in Italy, with him as my co-advisor. We worked with soil moisture as a natural rain gauge, estimating rainfall from how soils wet up, and realized the same idea could reveal irrigation.

After my PhD in 2019 I kept building collaborations around irrigation. Work with Ronnie Abolafia-Rosenzweig and Narendra Das led to a postdoc at Michigan State University and my move to the United States in 2022. An invitation from Sujay Kumar then brought me to NASA Goddard.
